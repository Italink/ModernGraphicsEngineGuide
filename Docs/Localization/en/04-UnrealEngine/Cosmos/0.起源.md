# Cosmos Development Log - Origin

For developers, creating products that receive praise from users is both a dream and a mission.

However, products vary in scale. When the goal involves a grand business landscape or complex technical architecture, relying solely on the meager strength of individual developers is like a mayfly trying to shake a tree.

As project scale expands exponentially, we have to pile up a large number of human resources to increase productivity. In this庞大 industrial assembly line, every participant is alienated into a standardized part on a precision instrument, losing creative autonomy in the interlocking gears of processes, deeply trapped by requirement iterations and KPI assessments, and gradually reduced to a cold statistical unit in the digital torrent.

Take the UP as an example. In recent years, the UP's work has mainly focused on pipelines (digging big holes) and optimization (cleaning up messes). Although this is a relatively core responsibility, it is undeniable that it has also made the UP drift further away from the original intention of pursuing product content and user experience, and even slowly lost the initial motivation to learn technology.

## Positioning

Against this backdrop, after countless decisions, the UP made up his mind and secured what might be the last long period of "free time" in his current life stage to create an independent "work" — **Cosmos**.

- Cosmos is a metaverse demonstration Demo built on Unreal Engine.

In terms of effects, the UP will iterate as much as possible toward a stunning user experience.

In terms of technology, the technical system of Cosmos will definitely be very hardcore.





It carries many of the UP's expectations.

It carries many of the UP's expectations, but first, apologies are in order: Cosmos is not a continuation of Specinker. The reason for abandoning it is straightforward:

- Audio visualization is no longer a novelty these days. When the UP first started development, apart from spectrum visualization in professional software, most user-side tools used Rainmeter. However, it was too outdated and had great limitations, which is why the UP chose to develop Specinker — it was interesting and challenging. But now, it seems that you can see spectrums wherever audio is involved. For example, some players now have spectrums, there are many more spectrum tools on Steam, and some are even better and more dazzling.
